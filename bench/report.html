What the wallpaper uses, and what it ignores

Uses the beat clock. Every hard hit lands on a predicted beat, so a hit can never land off the beat, and when the clock isn't sure (breaks, ambient, talk) it goes loose and hard hits stop. Each beat carries an accent (how hard that beat position has been hitting over the last bars) so the visuals punch harder on the beats that really slam.
Ignores kick and snare labels for hard hits. Telling a kick from a bass note or a snare from a clap in a full mix is the weak part: fired kicks are right 70% of the time (12.26/min wrong), snares only 28%. That is exactly the phantom-hit problem you were worried about, so they only shade the colour of an effect, never trigger one.
Drops fire the big shatter only on a jump of at least 8 dB in loudness and bass after a quieter stretch, then wait 30 s. It misses some drops rather than shattering on a fake one.

Your K3 speakers

Your hand-fixed Adrenaline kicks sit +242 ms after the drum stem's kicks (48 of your 56 kicks line up within 25 ms at that offset, measured against the low end's onset, which itself trails the click by ~25 ms), so about 250 to 265 ms after the attack. That is the Bluetooth delay you heard them through. The listener waits that long before sending hits whenever the K3 speakers are the output, and sends them immediately on the laptop speakers or wired output.
